# 10 — Rules that protect your accounts, scams to ignore, privacy

Accounts on Fiverr and Upwork take months to build and minutes to lose. Read each platform's
Terms of Service yourself; these are the rules that most often get freelancers banned.

## Platform rules

- **Stay on the platform** with clients you met there: no e-mail, phone, WhatsApp or outside
  payment before or during an order. Use the platform's messages, custom offers and milestones.
- **No contact details or external links** in gig descriptions, images or early messages.
- **Reviews:** never offer discounts, gifts or extras in exchange for a review, never review
  yourself, never ask friends to buy to leave reviews.
- **One account per person.** Do not let anyone else use your account.
- **Honest portfolio:** show only work you made or have permission to show.
- **AI tools:** you may use AI tools in your workflow, but you are responsible for the result.
  Be truthful if a client asks how you work, and follow any AI-disclosure option the platform
  offers in your category.

## Intellectual property

- Your games use **original names, code and graphics** — keep it that way. Never name or
  market a game with someone else's trademark (game titles, studio names, characters).
- Client logos and photos: use them **only for that client's project**, and only with their
  permission. Spec demos for prospects use their name and colours only, sent privately.
- Fonts and assets you add later must have a license that allows commercial use.

## Promotions, prizes and privacy (your clients' responsibility, your advice)

- Prize wheels and "win" games can fall under **promotion / sweepstakes rules** in some
  countries. Keep the legal line ("No purchase necessary…", validity, one per customer) and
  tell clients to adapt it to local law.
- Lead capture collects personal data: the client needs a consent checkbox, a privacy policy
  link (`termsUrl`) and a lawful place to store the data (GDPR in Europe, similar laws elsewhere).
- Never keep client leads yourself after delivery.

## Scams to ignore

- "Send your Telegram/WhatsApp, the project manager will interview you" → off-platform scam.
- Clients who pay by **cheque** or send "too much" and ask for a refund of the difference.
- "Install this app / extension / file to see the job details" → malware.
- Unpaid "test tasks" that are actually the full job.
- "Pay a fee to get the job" or for "training" / "equipment".
- Crypto payments for normal web work, or anyone who pushes you to hurry outside the platform.

## Working safely with client code

- Open unknown projects in a separate folder; read `package.json` scripts before running
  anything; use `npm install --ignore-scripts` on untrusted code.
- Never paste client secrets (API keys, passwords) into public places or repositories.
- Keep a backup of every delivery (zip + date) for at least 3 months.
