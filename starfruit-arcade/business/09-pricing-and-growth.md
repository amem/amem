# 09 — Pricing and growth

Start low enough to win the first reviews, then raise prices in steps as proof builds up.
All launch prices below match `catalog.json`, the website and the gig docs.

## 2. Extras that raise the average order

| Extra | Price | Why it sells |
|---|---|---|
| Extra-fast delivery (1 day sooner) | +$30 | Events and launches have deadlines |
| Extra language version | +$20 per game | Tourist areas, bilingual countries |
| Hosting setup + custom link | +$25 | Most small businesses have no web person |
| Lead capture to Google Sheets | +$40 (if not in the package) | Emails are worth more than the game |
| Seasonal re-theme (Christmas, Ramadan, summer…) | +$35 | Recurring, easy, high value |
| Printable QR table tent (PDF) | +$15 | Physical shops need it |
| Ad SDK integration (game devs) | +$60 | Required for portals |

## 3. When to raise prices

- **After 3–5 five-star reviews** on Fiverr: +20 % on all packages.
- **When you are booked more than 5 days ahead:** +20 % again, or remove the Basic package.
- **After 10 orders:** add a premium "Agency" package (3 games, 3 languages, white-label, $500+).
- **Products:** after the first 10 sales of an item, end the launch discount; after a big update,
  raise its price by $5 and announce the update.

Never lower prices to compete with the cheapest sellers. Improve the demo and the reply speed instead.

## 4. Growth levers, in order of impact

1. **Speed of reply and delivery** (platform ranking + word of mouth).
2. **Personal demos** for businesses (their logo in a playable game).
3. **Reviews** that mention results ("we collected 200 emails in a week").
   Ask clients, after a couple of weeks, how it went, and ask if you may quote them.
4. **Recurring revenue:** monthly care plans and seasonal themes for every business client.
5. **Agencies:** marketing agencies resell games to their clients. Offer them white-label
   (no credit line, `studioCredit: ''`) at a wholesale price with the Extended license.
6. **New products from what clients ask for:** the scratch card, quiz and advent calendar are
   already in the kit; next ideas are a "find the hidden item" photo game, a loyalty stamp card
   and a wedding/event trivia night. Build it once for a client (with their agreement on
   ownership), then turn it into a product.
7. **Sell by the calendar:** pitch each seasonal campaign 4–6 weeks before the date (see
   [README.md](README.md), "Sell by the calendar"). Every December calendar client is a
   Ramadan, summer or Black Friday client later in the year.
8. **Content:** one useful video or post per week compounds over months.

## 5. Simple targets

These are goals to steer by, not promises. Results vary a lot by market and effort.

| Period | Goal |
|---|---|
| Week 1 | All accounts live, 20 businesses listed, 5 personal demos sent |
| Month 1 | First 3–5 paid orders, first reviews, 10+ product page visits a day |
| Month 2 | 2 orders a week, first recurring client, first portal submission |
| Month 3 | Prices raised once, 1 agency partner, a new product from client demand |

## 6. Track it

Sales log columns: *date · channel · client/product · package · price · fee · net · hours spent · source (how they found you) · review yes/no*.

Every month look at: net income per hour by channel, which gig converts best, which game or
post brought traffic. Spend next month's time where the money per hour is highest.
