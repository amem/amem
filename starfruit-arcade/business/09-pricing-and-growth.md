# 09 — Pricing and growth

## Price ladder

| Stage | When | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Launch | 0–5 reviews | Launch prices (Fiverr tables), deliver early, over-communicate |
| Established | 5–20 reviews, ≥ 4.9 stars | Regular prices; add extras (fast delivery, extra game, translations) |
| In demand | Orders queue up | +20 % every time you are booked > 1 week ahead; raise minimum order |

Platform fees: Fiverr and Upwork keep a share of each payment (check the current rates in your
account), and stores charge per sale. Price so the **net** amount is worth your time.

## Upsells that clients actually buy

- A second game or the hub page ("make it an arcade")
- Lead capture connected to their mailing tool
- Seasonal reskins (Christmas, Ramadan, summer, Black Friday) — same game, new prizes and colours
- Monthly "promo refresh": new prizes/colours each month for $30–60/month
- Hosting and analytics setup
- Portal SDK integration for game developers

## Illustrative targets (not a promise)

These show how the numbers add up if you execute the daily routine. Real results vary widely
with effort, niche demand, timing and luck.

| Month | Activity | What success could look like |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Launch, 100+ outreach messages, 40+ proposals | First 2–5 orders, first reviews, first store sales |
| 2 | Regular prices, repeat clients, weekly videos | 5–10 orders, a few store sales each week |
| 3 | Agency partners, seasonal campaigns | Steady order flow; decide what to double down on |

## What to measure weekly

- Messages sent → replies → orders (conversion at each step)
- Gig impressions → clicks → orders (Fiverr analytics)
- Proposals sent → interviews → hires (Upwork)
- Store page views → sales (itch.io / Gumroad analytics)
- Hours spent per order (raise prices where it's high)

## Growth moves (months 2–6)

1. Turn every delivered client game into a **case study** (with permission): before/after,
   what it achieved, a quote.
2. Build one **new product per month** (quiz game, scratch card, runner) — each is a new gig, a
   new store item and a new portfolio piece.
3. Partner with 2–3 agencies as their white-label game developer.
4. Publish your best game on 2 portals for ad revenue.
5. Keep platform clients on their platform; build your own website traffic for direct clients.
